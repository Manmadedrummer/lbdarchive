Sweet sounds benefit Relay for Life

STEPHEN GEINOSKY
Updated June 8, 2008, 7:02 a.m. CT
Share to Facebook Share to Twitter Share by email

Abingdon's first Cupcake Music Festival almost found itself rained out Saturday afternoon.

"It had me scared there for a little bit," said Tyler Gordon, band coordinator. "It started sprinkling and we tarped up the amps. ... We have a rain alternative; we were going to go down to the fairgrounds."

But with the weather holding out, music fans flocked to the festival, a benefit for the American Cancer Society.

"It's actually a tribute to the most basic form of a fundraiser, which is a bake sale," said organizer Drew Fitch. "That's what the day is, we're raising money for cancer. Plus, who doesn't smile when you think of cupcakes."

A memorial board was available for people who have been personally affected by cancer. Visitors could write a short message on the board, which will be displayed at the Knox County Relay for Life.

All proceeds collected Saturday go toward the ACS, and a check will be handed over to the group during Relay for Life.

Gordon said the organizers are not sure if they will continue to give the proceeds to the ACS in possible future Cupcakes, or if they will alternate charities.

Organizers held the festival in Fitch's hometown to showcase Abingdon to other communities.

"This town really needs a kick in the pants," Fitch said. "I really hope that today we're giving it."

The concert did attract people from outside of Abingdon.

Concert-goers and vendors were also excited about the impact Cupcake had on Abingdon and the surrounding area.

Bonnie Froelich, Rotarian and member of the Rotary Club's Blue Ribbon Committee, and other Rotary members helped set up the festival Saturday.

"Abingdon needs to get on the map somehow, and this just might help," Froelich said. "The more people in town the better.

"Downtown obviously has some problems, but it's still a usable space. Maybe someone will come in and see a building in good shape."

Janelle Durbin, Monmouth, took part in one of the festival's many cupcake eating contests.

"Give it some time, give it a couple years and people will hear about it and know about it," Durbin said. "They'll come flying."

Organizers began discussions on Cupcake three months ago. While originally meant for only four bands, Gordon said the idea eventually exploded to 12.

"We saw that it could go huge," he said.

At one point, 50 bands were being considered for the festival, some from as far away as Iowa. Gordon found many of the rock, indie, alternative and metal bands through MySpace.

Fitch and Gordon were excited about the reaction from the crowd.

"Everybody here has a very positive attitude," Gordon said. "The energy here is awesome."

"We're trying to make it an annual event," Gordon said. "I'm sure we won't have any problem as long as things go smoothly for the rest of the day."
