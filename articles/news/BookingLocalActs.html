COLUMNS

Booking local acts

Steve Southard
March 10, 2011 | Updated March 11, 2011, 9:15 a.m. CT
Share to Facebook Share to Twitter Share by email

Hello readers and welcome back to another edition of Galesburg After Dark.

The past few years have been trying for many residents of this nation, and Galesburg has felt the effects of a dwindling economy just as everywhere else. Local businesses have dropped hours, changed locations and even adopted entirely new business models in an effort to stay afloat during the recession. With every man, woman, and child thinking about their bottom line, many of our personal "extras" have taken a backseat to daily living essentials. Factor in the ascension of cheap, accessible "stay-at-home" entertainment and there seems to be even less of an incentive for anyone to leave their house beyond their usual 9-5 workday.

This lack of an audience has influenced a more "back to basics" approach amongst both business owners and music promoters. Many after-hours establishments throughout the mid-west have cut down on taking financial chances with live music, leading them to stick with traditional bands that cover your favorite songs rather than gamble on a lesser known group with original material. Independent bands have seen a sharp decrease in demand, and most are more likely to land a show in someone's basement or garage before stepping foot on a real stage for a soundcheck.

At the end of the day, it all makes sense when it comes down to the numbers. Money always talks and I see no shame in a business keeping their patrons content. However, if history has taught me anything, it is that everything comes and goes in an almost cyclical nature. One person who has experienced many of the highs and lows of the modern music scene is Andy Greiner. Andy has spent most of his life living in the area and has recently taken a hold of the booking aspect of shows, helping to try and merge the numerous worlds of music found within this town. The booking team is currently trying out a new venue in the 21 Club, a move that has brought about much criticism and negativity amongst the already shallow numbers of loyal supporters. I recently had a chance to talk with Mr. Greiner about his rise to booking, and his team's move in an effort to shed some light on this situation.

To start, could you tell our readers a bit about yourself?

My full name is Andrew Taz Robert Greiner. I am 18 years old and was born in San Diego, CA. My family moved to the area about 15 years ago, largely due to the fact that my parents grew up in Peoria. I attend Carl Sandburg College and work at Hy-Vee on Main St. My free time consists of music and watching baseball with my father. Go Cardinals!

I know you to be a well rounded musician. What instruments are you able to play and what caused you to start?

Well yes, I do play a few. I've had proper training in guitar, string bass, tuba, trombone and the piano. Other than that I've learned how to play around on the drums, banjo and the fife on my own. My first major influence was the music of Green Day. Their album "American Idiot" was the third album I purchased after the Beatles' "number 1's" and Good Charlotte's "Young and the Hopeless". I instantly fell in love with the sound and soon thereafter picked up an electric guitar from MusicMakers, and started lessons under the tutelage of former Mindefect guitarist Brian Glasgow. Glasgow also helped me get into the music scene as a whole.

What local acts have you been associated with in the past? Are you currently putting together any new music?

I helped start my first band, Right Turn Down Wrong Street, as a freshman in high school. I handled the bass duties and we played a few shows in Monmouth, Galesburg, and Burlington. After adding a guitar player, a change in musical direction and a change of name, I decided I needed some time away from the group. I later re-joined as With Smoke Inhaled's 3rd guitar, played a few shows and once again found myself unhappy. After taking a few years off, I decided to give music another chance and helped form a band called EDEN, which I would take on the duties of singer, songwriter and lead guitar. We played a good number of shows around Galesburg, Abingdon, and Kewanee, and eventually recorded a demo with Drew Webster. Sadly, the band ended up calling it quits on the night of our cd release. As of right now, I am the singer and guitarist of the Paige and the bass player of the folky, alternative band the Rootabaga Jammers.

Would you please outline your road to booking shows?

My first taste of booking was while I was in my first band. We booked the Monmouth Park and a few shows at the Armoury just to secure some playing time. After I left the band, I basically only went to shows to support my guitar teacher, Brian Glasgow and his band Mindefect. While going to those shows I met the man in charge of booking at the time, Mike West, and became a sort of understudy to his ways. When Mike decided to step down and Jake Dillon's TTE began to take over, Jake approached me to help with the promotion of his shows. After the final TTE show on new year's eve of this year, I decided to book a night showcasing the new bands of the area in an attempt to usher in a new year of music, but the closing of Billiards in early January had us crambling for a new venue. Corey McDowell, one of my booking partners in crime and a member of Woodhull rockers Lethal by Default, called the owner of the 21 Club and scheduled a tour of the building. After a quick visit, we scheduled two shows, one in January and one in February. Things have been going well on our end so we are going to see how things go into the summer.

What have been some of the positives and negatives of moving your operation the the 21 Club?

The biggest positive is the fact that a lot of people still think of the local scene as strong and thriving. We've only done two shows up to this point, and other than wishing for higher attendance, everything has went very well. The fans and the bands are still willing to make this work and that is the biggest factor for me. The only negative I've noticed is the overall bad publicity that has surrounded the 21 Club in recent years. Everyone believes that as soon as they step inside, they will be either shot, stabbed, or robbed, and that is just not the case. I think people love to buy into hype without looking for themselves, and that's really all that we are asking. Come see for yourself before you start to trash our new beginning. Personally, I've been up and down to all three floors and felt safe on every one. The food is pretty good too!

There does seem to be an overwhelming negative opinion towards the place for whatever reason. What are you doing as a promoter to help change that mindset?

I really hope to totally break that opinion. The March 19th show has been set-up to let EVERYONE get a taste of what we are doing. The show will be free and we have a line-up that should appeal to each of the different crowds around town. We are going to give some of the Buddes' regular bands a chance in a new venue and see how that goes along with the old guard of the Fierce Amateurs. If you want to form your own opinion, come to that show. If you are sincerely worried about your well being, then please let it be known that there is security on hand that does not leave the concert floor. Thankfully, we haven't had to use them yet, but it is something we have never had anywhere else. In my mind, that is an added bonus.

The Galesburg music scene has ups and downs just as anything else. What is your opinion of the current scene? What would you attribute to these peaks and valleys and what do you think can be done to help the situation?

In the few years that I have been around, I have definately seen these peaks and valleys that you speak of. My personal favorite time for local acts was watching the rise of A Hill to Die Upon throughout 2007 and 2008 because it really felt like I was watching something special. I think the swell and decline in the scene has a lot to do with things getting stale and people getting bored with the same acts. Shows in the area will showcase the same band over a dozen times a year, and no new bands get a chance to get on these shows. If your act plays Galesburg, you are always welcome back, but very rarely do we see a chance for a totally new band to come in and secure a spot. 2010 was a different year in that aspect because we actually brought 10 totally new acts into town, but when you are booking 25-30 shows a year with 5 bands per show, it doesn't add up to much. Also, there seems to be a very different feeling and atmosphere to each of the venues and the bands that play there. I go to Budde's Pizza three or four times a month for the open mic nights and I always see the same 25 faces, than I head to the Saturday night show, wherever that may be, and see the same 70 faces, only maybe 5 of which attend both. I see a large portion of people looking down at other acts for a myriad of reasons, and talking down other local music and none of it makes sense. We are all in this together and I don't understand why you would want to cut down someone's art. I think the best way to break out of this lull is to promote these shows to the fullest extent and let people make up their own minds. The bands are willing, but the people of Galesburg need to know about it.

In conclusion, what have you been listening to lately?

My player is filled with the Avett Brothers, Mumford and Sons, Wilson, the newest Darkest Hour, Social Distortion and Cannonball Adderly.

The next two shows scheduled at the 21 Club are as follows:

Saturday, April 9th with A Hill to Die Upon, Ageless, Target the Messenger and Ashes of Avarice. All Ages. more info coming soon.
